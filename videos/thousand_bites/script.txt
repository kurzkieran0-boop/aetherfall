IN THIS TOWN, YOU GET 1,000 BITES
(narrator: Maya; voice en_US-amy-medium; CAPS = red caption words)

1. In this town, everyone is only allowed one thousand bites of food in their entire life.
2. You're born with a glowing number on your chest. Every bite counts it down.
3. When it hits zero, your teeth turn to STONE and you can never chew again.
4. And if you feed someone else, it counts against YOU. So nobody ever shares food.
5. Birthday cakes have one candle and one bite. Whoever gets it makes a speech.
6. Restaurants serve one grape per table. People stare at it for an hour.
7. The baker's best seller is a single crumb on a very nice plate. Twelve dollars.
8. School lunch is a bell, a tray, and a very long sigh.
9. And then there's me. Maya. Nine years old.
10. I ate everything. Pancakes. Pickles. A whole watermelon. Once, a sandwich with another sandwich inside it, because it was delicious.
11. Then on my birthday, Mom looked at my chest and went pale. Three hundred bites left.
12. She grabbed my face and whispered, "Chew slowly." Then she cried into a napkin, because crying is free.
13. So I became the smallest eater in town. One lentil at lunch. Half a pea at dinner. Kids called me Crumb Girl.
14. The only one who understood was my Grandma Rue. Every night she'd sneak into my room with a thermos and whisper, "Drink this."
15. It was her tomato soup. Hot. Salty. Perfect. I sipped it slowly under my blanket.
16. And my counter never moved. I thought that was strange. I didn't think about why.
17. Grandma Rue only had four bites left. She never ate in public. She told everyone she wasn't hungry.
18. Then one morning, at the town parade, Grandma Rue collapsed. Right in the middle of Main Street.
19. The whole town gathered around. Everyone stared. Nobody moved. Nobody wanted to waste a bite.
20. The doctor whispered, "She needs real food. Right now."
21. The baker held up a crumb, then put it back. The mayor looked at his shoes.
22. So I ran to the bakery window, grabbed a loaf, tore off a piece, and put it in Grandma's mouth.
23. Three hundred. Two ninety-nine. Two fifty.
24. Every bite I fed her took one from me.
25. Two hundred. One fifty. One hundred. Fifty. Ten. Five. Three. Two. One. ZERO.
26. My teeth went cold. Then heavy. Then solid STONE. I would never chew again.
27. But Grandma still wouldn't swallow. The bread was too dry. She was too weak.
28. Her eyes opened a tiny bit. She whispered one word. "Soup."
29. And suddenly I remembered. Every night. The thermos. The blanket. The counter that never moved.
30. The counter only counts BITES. Not sips.
31. I pulled the thermos out of my backpack and held it to her lips. She drank. Slowly. And the counter stayed at zero.
32. Color came back to her face. She smiled and whispered, "I wondered when you'd figure it out."
33. The whole town watched with open mouths. Then the baker ran for a pot. Then the mayor ran for a ladle.
34. By sunset, there was soup on every corner. Pumpkin. Chicken. Potato. A hundred spoons clinking, and not one counter moving.
35. The next morning, the restaurants served soup. The bakery sold soup. Even birthday cake got blended.
36. And me? I can't chew anymore. But I've never been so full.
37. In this town, everyone drinks their dinner.
