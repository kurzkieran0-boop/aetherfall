IN THIS TOWN, YOU GET 5,000 WORDS
(narrator: Finn; voice en_US-ryan-high; CAPS = red caption words)

1. In this town, everyone is only allowed five thousand words in their entire life.
2. You're born with a glowing number on your chest. Every word you say counts it down.
3. When it hits zero, your voice turns to STONE.
4. So nobody here wastes a single word.
5. The mayor's speeches are four words long. "Roads fixed. Taxes up."
6. Weddings are two words. "I do." And everyone claps, because clapping is free.
7. The baker doesn't say good morning. He just holds up a loaf and nods.
8. Kids learn hand signals before they learn to read. There's a sign on every corner: TALK ONLY IN EMERGENCIES.
9. And then there's me.
10. I talked about everything. I talked to the dog. I talked to the trees. I talked to the toaster, because the toaster never interrupted.
11. I talked because talking was fun.
12. Then, on my birthday, my mom looked at my chest and her face went white. Three hundred words left.
13. She grabbed my shoulders and used four of her own words just to say, "Finn. Stop."
14. So I stopped talking. I carried a whiteboard everywhere. Kids called me Whiteboard Boy.
15. At recess, everybody whispered and signed and gossiped with their eyebrows, and I sat there, saving my three hundred words for something that mattered.
16. The only thing I did for fun was hum. Quietly. In the shower. In my closet. Under my blanket.
17. And the counter never moved. I thought that was strange. I didn't think about why.
18. Then one afternoon, Mom took me and my baby brother Pip to the lake.
19. Pip was giggling at the ducks, in a little rowboat tied to the dock.
20. Mom turned around for one second to pay for ice cream. By pointing.
21. The rope slipped. The boat started to drift. Faster. And faster. Toward the waterfall.
22. Mom ran to the end of the dock, but she only had eleven words left. She couldn't scream.
23. Everybody on the shore froze. Nobody wanted to waste a word.
24. One man whispered, "Help." His counter flashed red. He closed his mouth and looked away.
25. So I dropped my whiteboard. I took a breath. And I yelled, "PIP! GRAB THE OAR! PADDLE LEFT!"
26. Three hundred. Two fifty. Two hundred. Every word hit my chest like a punch.
27. Pip heard me. He paddled. But the waterfall kept getting closer.
28. One fifty. One hundred. Fifty. Ten. Five. Three. Two. One. ZERO.
29. My throat went cold. Then heavy. My voice turned to solid STONE. I opened my mouth and nothing came out.
30. The boat was thirty feet from the edge. Pip was crying. I couldn't scream. I couldn't warn anyone.
31. And then I remembered the shower. The closet. The blanket. The counter never moved when I hummed.
32. The counter only counts WORDS. Not songs.
33. So I sang. "Pip, paddle left. Paddle left. You can do it." And the counter stayed at zero.
34. Pip stopped crying. He paddled left, in time with the song.
35. Mom grabbed my hand and sang with me. Then the baker. Then the mayor. Then the whole town.
36. A hundred people sang the same four words, and Pip paddled to the beat, and the boat turned, and bumped the dock one foot from the waterfall.
37. Pip reached up, laughing. Mom hugged us both so hard she cried.
38. After that, everything changed. The next morning, the mayor gave his speech. In a song.
39. The baker sold bread in harmony. The kids sang their lessons. Grandma sang insults at the neighbors.
40. In this town, everyone SINGS.
