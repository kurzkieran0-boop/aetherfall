IN THIS TOWN, YOU GET 2,000 DOORS
(narrator: Theo; voice en_US-joe-medium; CAPS = red caption words)

1. In this town, everyone is only allowed two thousand doors in their entire life.
2. Every time you open a door, the glowing number on your chest counts down.
3. When it hits zero, every door in the world LOCKS for you. Forever.
4. So nobody here wastes a single door.
5. Families open the front door once, in the morning. Dinner is at the same time for everyone, because you only get one trip.
6. The bakery props its door open with a loaf of bread. That's why the bread is always stale.
7. The mailman doesn't knock. He throws letters through the slot and sprints before anyone gets ideas.
8. And the school has no doors inside. Just curtains. Very thick curtains.
9. And then there's me. Theo. Ten years old.
10. I opened every door. Closet doors. Fridge doors. The oven, twice, because it made a satisfying sound.
11. I loved doors. I wanted to know what was behind every single one of them.
12. Then, on my birthday, Dad looked at my chest and dropped his coffee. Three hundred doors left.
13. He said, "Theo. From now on, you use the front door twice a day. That's it."
14. So I became the kid who never leaves his room. I ate cereal on my bed. Kids called me Doorknob.
15. But at night, I snuck out. Not through the door. Through the little round window above my desk.
16. I did that every night for a year. And my counter never changed. Not once. I didn't think about why.
17. Then one windy afternoon at school, the alarm bell rang. Smoke. The old art room was on fire.
18. Every kid ran to the exit. One heavy front door. And nobody opened it.
19. The teachers whispered, "Wait. We have to decide who has the most doors left."
20. The smoke got thicker. Kids started coughing. A little girl named Lily was crying in the back.
21. So I ran to the front door. I grabbed the handle. And I pulled.
22. Three hundred. Two ninety. Every kid ran out. Then I ran back in. Classroom doors. Closet doors. The janitor's closet. Every kid I found, I pushed outside.
23. Two hundred. One fifty. One hundred. Fifty. Ten. Five. Three. Two. One. ZERO.
24. The last door clicked. And locked. Every door. Forever. Even the one I was standing behind.
25. I was trapped in the art room. Smoke everywhere. The handle wouldn't turn. I couldn't open anything.
26. I pounded on the door. Nothing. And then I looked up. And I saw it. The little round window above the paint shelves.
27. The counter only counts DOORS. Not windows.
28. I climbed the shelves. I pushed it open. And I jumped out into the grass, coughing, alive.
29. Every kid was outside. The teachers. The parents. The whole town. All staring at the little round window, and at me.
30. The school was saved by a bucket line, passed through every window in the building.
31. After that, everything changed. The next morning, the mayor cut a giant window into the town hall. Then the baker cut one into his bakery.
32. Soon nobody used doors at all. Kids climbed into school through windows. The mailman delivered letters by ladder. Grandma hosted tea parties on her roof.
33. And me? I've never been locked out of anything again.
34. In this town, everyone uses WINDOWS.
(hard cut to black)
