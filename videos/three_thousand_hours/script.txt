IN THIS TOWN, YOU GET 3,000 HOURS AWAKE
(narrator: Nora; voice en_US-kristin-medium; CAPS = red caption words)

1. In this town, everyone is only allowed three thousand hours awake in their entire life.
2. You're born with a glowing number on your chest. Every hour your eyes are open, it counts down.
3. When it hits zero, your eyelids turn to STONE and never open again.
4. So nobody here wastes a single hour.
5. The mayor holds meetings from bed. The baker sells bread in his pajamas. The mailman delivers letters with his eyes shut, and a very patient dog.
6. Kids do homework with their eyes closed. They get a lot of questions wrong. Nobody minds.
7. The school bell is a gentle lullaby, because nobody wants to wake up too fast.
8. And then there's me. Nora. Eleven.
9. I stayed up all night. I watched fireflies. I counted stars. I read under my blanket with a flashlight, because I had to know how the book ended.
10. Sleeping felt like missing something.
11. Then, on my birthday, Mom looked at my chest and went pale. Three hundred hours left.
12. After that, she tucked me in at seven every night and read me the most boring story in the world. Kids called me Sleepy Nora. I was not sleepy.
13. But I had a secret game. Every night, I'd sneak out with my eyes closed and walk the whole town in the dark. Every street. Every turn. Every step, by heart.
14. And my counter never moved on those walks. I thought that was strange. I didn't think about why.
15. Then one stormy night, I woke up to a strange sound. Thunder. And water. A lot of water.
16. The river had burst. The whole town was flooding. And everyone was asleep.
17. The mayor. The baker. Every family. All safe in bed, with their counters paused on their chests.
18. I ran to the bell tower and rang it as loud as I could.
19. Some people opened one eye. They looked at their counters. They looked at the water. And they closed it again. Nobody wanted to spend an hour.
20. So I went house to house, banging on every door. Three hundred. Two fifty. Two hundred. The longer I stayed awake, the less time I had left.
21. One fifty. One hundred. Fifty. Ten. Five. Three. Two. One. ZERO.
22. My eyelids went cold. Then heavy. Then solid STONE. The world went black.
23. I stood there in the flood, completely blind, with a whole town asleep behind me.
24. And then I remembered. The game. The streets. Every step, by heart.
25. I didn't need my eyes. I never had.
26. I walked. Knocking on doors. Pulling people out of bed. "Follow me. Hold hands. Eyes shut."
27. Mom grabbed my hand. Then the baker. Then the mayor. Then the whole town, in one long line, eyes closed, wading through the flood.
28. I led them through the dark, street by street, up the hill, to the old stone church on the high ground.
29. When the sun came up, the water was gone. The town was soaked. And everyone was alive.
30. And me? I'll never see a sunrise again. But I walked a whole town to safety without opening my eyes once.
31. After that, everything changed. Every night, the whole town plays my game. Eyes closed. Hands held.
32. The mayor leads the way. The baker counts steps. Grandma narrates, badly.
33. In this town, everyone sees with their HANDS.
