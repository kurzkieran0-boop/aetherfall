THE MAILMAN SKIPS OUR HOUSE
(narrator: Eli; CAPS = red caption words)

1. The mailman has skipped our house for forty days. And every time he walks past, he looks SCARED.
2. My name is Eli. I'm eleven. Our town has exactly one mailman, Mr. Dobbs, and he has never missed a house. Not once. Not in thirty years.
3. Until day one. He walked up our street, stopped at our gate, looked at our mailbox, and kept walking.
4. I figured it was a mistake. Day two, same thing. Day three, he crossed the street to avoid us.
5. By day ten, he was walking faster. By day twenty, he was running. By day thirty, he wore his cap pulled down over his eyes, like he didn't want our house to see his face.
6. Everyone else got mail. The baker got bills. Mrs. Pell got postcards from her sister. The mayor got a letter every single morning, and he always read it standing in the street, and he always went pale.
7. Our mailbox got nothing. Just dust and one very confused spider.
8. So I asked Mom. She said, "Maybe nobody writes to us." Dad said, "Eat your soup." Which is what Dad says when he's lying.
9. That's when I knew something was up. So I made a plan.
10. Plan one: ask him directly. I waited at the corner at six a.m. He saw me from a block away, turned around, and ran. A grown man. With a mailbag the size of a sheep. Running.
11. Plan two: follow him.
12. He went to the bakery. Dropped a letter. The mill. Dropped a letter. Town hall. Dropped three.
13. Then he stopped at the end of the road, opened his bag, and pulled out one more envelope. Cream colored. Red wax seal. He stared at it for a long time.
14. Then he looked straight at our house. And whispered, "Not yet."
15. He put the letter back. And walked away.
16. Not YET? What does "not yet" mean?
17. That night I snuck out. Mr. Dobbs lives in the tiny blue house behind the post office. The light was on. I crept up to the window.
18. Inside, the walls were covered in envelopes. Hundreds. Maybe thousands. Every one cream colored. Every one sealed in red wax.
19. And every one had a name on it. The baker. Mrs. Pell. The mayor. Even Mr. Dobbs himself.
20. There was a whole shelf with the name of every kid in town. Clara. Theo. Maya. Jonah. All of them.
21. And in the middle, alone on a little hook, one letter that said: ELI.
22. I almost fell off the windowsill. Then the door creaked open behind me.
23. "You weren't supposed to see that," said Mr. Dobbs. His hands were shaking.
24. "What are those letters?" I asked. He looked at the floor. "Every letter in this town is from the FUTURE."
25. "They show up on my doorstep a day before anyone writes them. Nobody knows who sends them."
26. "Mostly they're boring. Bills. Birthdays. Recipes. But sometimes they say what's going to happen to you."
27. "Last year I delivered a letter to old Mr. Harlan. It said his barn would burn. He laughed. The barn burned the next morning."
28. "So I stopped delivering the bad ones. But yours is the worst one I've ever seen."
29. My stomach dropped. "What does it say?"
30. "I can't tell you." "Then give it to me." "You don't want it." "Mr. Dobbs. It's MY letter. It's my house. It's my life."
31. He stared at me for a long time. Then he took the cream envelope with my name and put it in my hands.
32. The wax was warm. Like someone had just pressed it.
33. I broke it open. Inside was one short note, in handwriting I knew better than my own.
34. It said: "Dear Eli. By now you've followed the mailman, climbed his window, and broken the seal. Please don't turn around."
35. It was signed: "Eli."
36. I turned around.
37. Mr. Dobbs was gone. The door was wide open. And standing in the doorway, holding a cream envelope with Mr. Dobbs's name on it... was ME.
